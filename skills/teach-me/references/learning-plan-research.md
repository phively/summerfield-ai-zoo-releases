# Research-backed learning plans

Use this reference when creating a curriculum, training recommendation, or learning plan. The adaptive teaching workflow owns instructional sequencing and assessment; this reference owns the research prompt and the connection between findings and the plan.

## Formulate the starting prompt

Use the user's supplied objective, background, practical context, time budget, desired retention or transfer, and independence boundary. Include relevant accessibility or resource constraints already known. Ask only for missing information that could materially change the plan; otherwise state a limited assumption. Do not invent background, preferences, mastery, or completed experience.

When external research is needed, show one concise, tailored research prompt before performing it. Showing the prompt is not itself confirmation or an instruction for the user to run it elsewhere. Use the parent skill's research route and its confirmation rules. Carry any actual current-request or session authorization to omit research-scope confirmation, restricted to its stated scope; otherwise retain the existing checkpoint. Do not manufacture authorization by writing a skip instruction into the prompt. Respect explicit instructions to provide only a prompt, avoid research, or wait for review; do not execute research when the user requested preparation only.

Adapt this template rather than presenting unfilled placeholders:

> Research the subject-matter evidence needed to design a learning plan for [observable capability] in [practical context]. The learner's supplied background is [relevant background]; available time and constraints are [time, resources, accessibility], and the independence boundary is [what they should do independently versus with tools]. Supplied facts and assumptions are [distinguish them]. Check [exact consequential questions about prerequisites, concepts, techniques, resources, or current requirements], for [applicable date, version, jurisdiction, or medium]. Research-scope confirmation: [the user's actual explicit waiver and applicable scope, if present; otherwise retain the normal checkpoint]. Use primary sources and strong syntheses appropriate to these questions. Return supported findings with direct citations and source identities, material alternatives and uncertainty, and any unresolved question that changes the plan. Distinguish evidence about the subject from suggestions about instructional design. Stay within this bounded learning-plan request; return the findings to the plan's author.

Research proportionately. When a supplied-material or pure-reasoning plan needs no external research, build directly from the supplied material or derivation without adding a research prompt. Do not invoke research merely to satisfy the template. For a mixed plan, research only the separable evidence-dependent parts.

## Use the findings

- Identify which findings determine the concepts, prerequisites, resource choices, or important limitations. Keep decisive citations close to those choices; do not substitute a bibliography for applying the evidence.
- Build the requested plan as a deliverable, with observable capabilities, supported and independent practice, fading, revisiting, transfer, and progress evidence as appropriate. Do not force an interactive lesson or a generic Socratic interview.
- Preserve the distinction between a supported subject-matter claim and a design judgment about pacing, exercises, or sequence. Do not claim that a particular curriculum is optimal without evidence supporting that comparison.
- Resolve material contradictions before using them as foundations. When research or a needed source is unavailable, identify the affected choices as provisional and continue with reliable portions; do not invent references, current resource availability, or verification.
- Keep the prompt, findings, and plan in the current task unless saving is requested or otherwise authorized. A schedule in the plan is a recommendation unless an actual reminder has been created through an authorized mechanism.
