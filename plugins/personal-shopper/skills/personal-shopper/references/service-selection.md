# Service and contractor selection

Use this workflow for services, subscriptions, and contractors. No recipe or Meal Planner prerequisite applies. Research is self-contained using available search, browser, or connected tools; no other plugin is required.

## Establish the service brief

Identify the desired outcome, included work or deliverables, hard requirements, relevant geography or coverage, budget, timing, and user priorities. Ask only for missing information that could materially change eligibility, scope, applicable evidence, or the comparison. Reuse answers already given. When a missing jurisdiction or service area affects eligibility, ask and wait before making a location-dependent recommendation; do not guess one. A general overview may proceed when requested, with regional eligibility and current fees explicitly unresolved.

Apply current-task instructions and confirmed facts before applicable saved service-category preferences, then explicitly general preferences. A grocery store ranking never becomes a contractor preference. Hard requirements remain binding.

## Discover and compare alternatives

When a product or service is named, investigate evidence-based alternatives that meet the same need and the user's priorities. Honor a request to restrict the comparison to named options. Select the comparison axes from the use case rather than from provider popularity.

For a robo-investing inquiry about Vanguard, investigate relevant alternatives such as Betterment and Fidelity where evidence establishes matching service scope. Compare applicable offerings, geography, fees, minimums, advice, account features, and the user's needs. Names are examples, not a mandatory shortlist or a suitability judgment. Keep unverified fees and eligibility unknown; do not turn a service comparison into unsupported personalized investment advice.

For a Mullvad VPN inquiry, compare alternatives against the user's emphasis on privacy, transparency, or both. Examine consequential evidence such as data collection, account/payment requirements, audit scope and date, ownership, source availability, and transparency reporting as applicable. Separate provider statements from independent verification. Open source alone does not prove operational privacy; a popularity ranking or marketing claim does not establish either axis. Explain when an option meets one priority but lacks support for the other.

## Keep evidence attributable

For consequential facts, retain source identity or URL, checked date, source type, applicable service or offer, geography, and the fact it supports. Read the underlying source rather than treating a search snippet as verification. Label user-supplied documents as supplied evidence. Distinguish provider claims, authoritative records, independent assessments, estimates, conflicts, and unknowns. An unavailable source is an evidence gap; do not claim it was checked successfully or infer that a qualification or fee does not exist.

## Match scope and qualifications

Compare providers on matching deliverables and service geography, qualifications required for that work, material and labor scope, timing, exclusions, warranty or follow-up where relevant, and fees. Distinguish a provider's advertised price, an estimate, and a quote actually supplied for this job. Do not describe a website listing as a received quote.

For contractors, check the applicable official registry or other authoritative qualification record where available. Match the legal entity or license identifier, trade, jurisdiction, status, and relevant dates to this work; a similarly named business or a license in another trade or state is insufficient. Provider websites supply claims, not independent verification. Identify conflicts between a current authoritative record and marketing claims. Distinguish a verified license from unverified insurance, bonding, or other qualifications; do not infer one from another. If access fails, state exactly what remains unverified and continue a useful conditional comparison without calling the provider verified-qualified.

Normalize quote inclusions such as materials, labor, disposal, permits, taxes, travel, and required follow-up before comparing totals. Separate one-time, recurring, and usage-dependent charges, their billing period, commitment, and cancellation terms when relevant. Use [pricing and allocation](pricing-and-allocation.md) for the cost breakdown. Keep unknown excluded charges visible; a known subtotal is not an all-in total. Do not select the nominally cheaper provider solely on an incomparable headline price.

When comparing a project's permit costs or estimating its likely final cost, use [project costs](project-costs.md) for official jurisdiction evidence, inclusions, comparable completed outcomes, and the distinction between an ordinary estimate and a reference-class forecast.

Apply a confirmed service preference to this task immediately. Persist only when authorized, following [shopping records](shopping-records.md) and the existing native record contract. Do not create a project dataset or new authority merely to return an estimate. Report current-record and required-history outcomes separately; if one is unverified, the combined save is incomplete.

Present a requirements-based comparison with meaningful alternatives, scope and fee basis, evidence actually checked, tradeoffs, material unknowns, and the next useful check. Research and recommendations authorize no provider contact, quote request, booking, application, purchase, payment, or durable save.
