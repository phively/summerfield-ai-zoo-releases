# Project costs, permits, and reference classes

Use this workflow for project research, applicable permit costs, and forecasts of final costs. Combine it with [service selection](service-selection.md) when comparing contractors. Do not require project intake for ordinary retail or grocery shopping.

## Establish the project and cost basis

Identify the proposed work, location and responsible jurisdiction, scope and scale, timing, estimate maturity, and known base estimate. State the base's currency, price date, and inclusions: materials, labor, disposal, tax, permits, professional or utility fees, and any risk provision. Ask only for missing facts needed to determine applicability or calculate a supported amount. Reuse supplied answers; do not assume a location, valuation, permit requirement, or baseline.

Keep the ordinary cost build-up separate from an outside-view forecast. Classify each component as a supplied quote, observed fee, estimate, conditional amount, or unknown, with source and date. Count charges once using [pricing and allocation](pricing-and-allocation.md). Where only some costs are known, return a useful partial subtotal and explicit unknowns rather than an invented all-in total.

## Research applicable permits and fees

Find the responsible authority's current official rules and fee schedule for that location and work. Establish the jurisdiction, permit or trade category, effective date, applicable valuation or other inputs, formula or tiers, minimum charges, and included or separate reviews and inspections. Identify possible utility or other authority charges separately when relevant. An older third-party article does not override a current applicable official schedule. A newer date alone does not establish that a schedule covers this jurisdiction or project category.

Read and cite the actual official source, recording its identity or URL, checked date, effective date, applicability, and calculation basis. Distinguish a checked rule from a provisional interpretation. If jurisdiction or valuation is missing, ask the targeted question before asserting a firm fee; a formula can be shown conditionally when its basis is supported. If a source is unavailable, state the failed access and unresolved applicability or amount. Neither missing evidence nor an unknown fee means that no permit or fee exists.

Show the arithmetic for supported fee inputs. If a contractor quote already includes that permit allowance, identify it and avoid adding it again. When replacing an allowance with a verified fee, show the adjustment and retain unverified inspection, utility, or other charges as unknown. Research does not authorize a permit application, provider contact, booking, payment, or any external write.

## Build a defensible reference class

Use completed-project outcomes comparable to the proposed work. Explain the selection rule before choosing an uplift: project type, scope and scale, geography, delivery conditions, baseline estimate maturity, completion status, and cost inclusions. Identify the source and sample size, excluded observations and why, missing inputs, and selection or reporting bias. Do not select only projects that support a preferred forecast.

For raw data, pair each project's actual final cost with its matching original base estimate. Establish that both amounts describe comparable scope, price basis, currency, and estimate maturity. Normalize price dates, currencies, scope, and inclusions only with supported conversion inputs and a stated method; otherwise exclude incompatible rows or state why the class cannot support a quantitative forecast. Do not silently pool different currencies, unfinished projects, or early concepts with mature contracted estimates. Zero or missing original bases cannot supply a final/base ratio.

For each eligible pair compute `ratio = final cost / original base estimate`. Sort the ratios in ascending order. Use the nearest-rank empirical quantile: the rank is the ceiling of probability times sample size, counting from one. State the convention, retained sample size, selected ranks and ratios, and show reproducible arithmetic. If using a published distribution instead, identify its source, methodology, applicable baseline, and limits; do not claim to have recomputed unavailable observations.

For a proposed project with a matching base, multiply that base by the chosen empirical ratio to show a sample P50 or P80 where the evidence supports those labels. The matching base must exclude the risk provision being estimated. Do not add another contingency for the same risk, or add permit, material, or tax amounts already included in the base or final-cost observations. If a quote's embedded contingency cannot be separated, disclose that mismatch before applying an uplift. An empirical sample percentile is not a calibrated guarantee for the proposed project.

## Return an estimate that matches the evidence

Report the scope, price date and currency, base and inclusions, comparable-data source, selection and exclusions, sample size, normalization, method and arithmetic, and consequential limitations. Match numerical precision to the inputs; small or biased samples may not justify probability labels even when a numerical quantile can be computed.

When the class is inadequate, say what comparable outcomes or baseline inputs are missing. Return any supportable ordinary estimate with its source basis, partial subtotal or range, and unresolved costs. Do not relabel a quote, a scenario, a few unrelated projects, or an invented distribution as reference-class forecasting. Do not import a generic infrastructure uplift into a household project. Keep controlled arithmetic examples visibly synthetic and separate from real project evidence.

Return research and forecasts in the task response. Reuse existing scoped shopping records only for a separately authorized useful preference or evidence update, with native readback and truthful per-target outcomes. Do not create an outcome database or persist the project dataset merely because a forecast was requested.
