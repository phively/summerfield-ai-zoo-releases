# Product selection and compatibility

Use this workflow for direct requests to choose or compare products, including grocery products. It has no recipe or Meal Planner prerequisite. For a request to turn an approved meal plan into a grocery list, use [the grocery workflow](grocery-workflow.md) instead.

## Establish a useful brief

Identify the product category and intended use, hard requirements, budget, region, and any relevant category rules. Ask only for missing information that could materially change which products qualify or how they compare. Use the user's stated constraints as hard requirements; distinguish them from preferences such as brand, appearance, durability, or professional-grade features.

When a missing region, hard requirement, or fit detail could change which products qualify or what evidence applies, ask for it and wait before comparing or recommending products. Do not substitute an assumed location or requirement. If the user asks for a general overview without resolving it, label the overview as non-current and omit claims about live research, regional prices, and availability unless those facts were actually checked.

Apply information in this order:

1. Current-task instructions and confirmed facts for this purchase.
2. Relevant saved preferences for this product category.
3. Preferences the user has explicitly made general across categories.
4. Clearly labeled assumptions where no preference or fact is available.

Never broaden a category-specific preference into a general rule, and never let any preference defeat a hard requirement. Apply the legacy ordered-store preference only to grocery purchases, as described in [store preferences](store-preferences.md).

## Discover relevant alternatives

When the user names a product, establish the need it serves and investigate alternatives against the same hard requirements and stated priorities. Do not assume the named option is suitable or return a generic popularity list. Explain why each alternative serves the same need, which requirements it meets, and its material tradeoffs. Honor an explicit restriction such as “compare only these two”; do not add candidates outside that comparison.

## Evaluate fit and compatibility

- Use confirmed details about owned equipment, dimensions, interfaces, models, placement, and intended use only when they bear on the product. Do not assume an unrecorded or unconfirmed measurement.
- Compare the candidate's exact model and relevant published dimensions, standards, interfaces, load limits, and mounting or clearance requirements with the user's setup. Prefer the manufacturer's current technical documentation or other authoritative specification for compatibility.
- If the necessary setup dimension is unknown, identify each manufacturer-specified measurement point and explain how to measure it. Name the points, direction, units, and required clearance or tolerance. Compare each required measurement separately with the specification; do not treat one point as representative of others unless the manufacturer says so. Keep the conclusion provisional until all required values are confirmed.
- For front-fender clearance, with the tire inflated to its intended riding pressure, explain measuring the smallest gap from the tire top to the underside of the fork crown in millimeters, in addition to each manufacturer-specified fork-leg point. Request these measurements separately. Apply a published minimum only to the points it covers; do not invent a crown minimum or assume fit when it is unspecified.
- Reject a candidate whose authoritative specification conflicts with a hard constraint, even if it costs less or ranks higher by preference. Explain the conflicting specification and do not describe that candidate as compatible.
- When sources disagree, identify the conflict and use the most authoritative applicable specification for fit. Do not convert a lack of contradictory evidence into proof of compatibility.

## Assess product evidence

Use manufacturer or standards-body materials for dimensions, interfaces, required labels, and technical claims. For evaluative claims such as reliability, comfort, or performance, prefer professional reviews that are independent and nonsponsored. Use retailer descriptions and customer reviews as secondary evidence, and identify material disagreement, limited coverage, or missing independent review evidence.

Retrieve applicable first-party technical documentation for the exact variant before making consequential hardware specification claims. Identify the manufacturer, model or part number, revision where relevant, dimensions, and selected options; a family-level page or similarly named retailer item may describe a different variant. Select technical fields from the application rather than copying an irrelevant specification list. For an M10 bolt, relevant fields can include thread pitch and length, dimensions, material, coating, strength or property class and applicable standard, and pack quantity. Nominal diameter alone does not establish interchangeability. Do not recommend a load-critical replacement as confirmed suitable while the required strength or application constraints are unknown.

Keep source URL or document identity, checked date, exact variant, and consequential facts together. Distinguish technical specifications from independent review judgments, retailer offers, and the user's confirmed setup. Read the source itself; finding a search result is not a completed specification lookup. If manufacturer access fails, report the failed attempt, any secondary source actually used, and each unresolved field. Do not invent a manufacturer lookup, quote, specification, or exact compatibility. Reject a conflicting retailer variant instead of averaging or silently mixing its fields with the manufacturer's variant.

Tie each recommendation to the user's requirements and explain meaningful model tradeoffs. When it affects the choice, compare consumer and business or professional models, likely service life, repairability, weather suitability, fit, and sizing. Do not imply that a higher price or professional label alone establishes better fit or durability.

## Compare prices and offers

Prefer a current price for the matching product and model in the user's region. Keep prices comparable by recording the source, date checked, currency, package or model identity, and relevant offer conditions. Identify material exclusions such as tax, delivery, required accessories, deposits, or membership.

Use the source hierarchy and confidence guidance in [pricing and allocation](pricing-and-allocation.md) when direct product prices need estimates or fallbacks.

Use a member, loyalty, coupon, or other conditional discount only when eligibility is confirmed for this task or an applicable saved record. Otherwise show the regular price when available and label the conditional offer with its requirement; do not treat it as the user's eligible price. Distinguish an observed matching-region price from an estimate or a price from another region. If current research was unavailable or incomplete, state that limit instead of implying that research occurred.

Keep a task-specific store instruction for this purchase unless the user explicitly authorizes saving or replacing a preference. Report a current-task override without claiming it changed saved preferences.

## Return useful recommendations

For each leading option, state how it meets the hard requirements, its material tradeoffs, compatibility status, price basis, and uncertainty. Separate confirmed fit from provisional fit. When evidence does not resolve an important question, identify the specific missing specification, measurement, offer condition, or price and give the next useful step.
