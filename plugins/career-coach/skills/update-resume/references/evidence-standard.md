# Evidence Standard

Use this standard to protect factual accuracy and claim traceability.

## Evidence classes

- **Explicit fact:** Directly stated in a user-provided source or by the user.
- **Supported inference:** Logically or strongly implied by identified explicit facts.
- **Hypothesis:** Plausible interpretation that needs confirmation.
- **Unknown:** Available evidence cannot support a conclusion.

Only explicit facts may appear as unqualified resume claims. A supported inference may appear without qualification only when the underlying facts logically establish the same meaning, scope, ownership, and outcome. Otherwise, place it in a confirmation queue.

## Prohibited strengthening

Do not alter evidence in ways that imply greater:

- ownership or decision authority,
- team, budget, customer, or geographic scope,
- causation or outcome,
- technical depth or hands-on use,
- tenure or years of experience,
- leadership level,
- certainty or exclusivity.

Changing “contributed to” into “led,” or an organizational outcome into an individually caused result, is a factual change rather than stylistic improvement.

## Source assessment

Prefer sources that are specific, first-hand, and contemporaneous to the work. Use dates and detail to interpret sources, but do not assume the newest source is automatically most accurate. Treat self-assessments, manager reviews, project records, resumes, and job descriptions as different perspectives rather than a fixed hierarchy.

When sources conflict:

1. Record the conflicting statements and their sources.
2. Determine whether the difference is scope, timing, terminology, or a true contradiction.
3. Treat a disputed value as unresolved unless one source explicitly supersedes or validates the other.
4. Do not choose one source solely because it is newer, more formal, or more detailed.
5. Use only the common supported meaning if it remains useful.
6. Ask for clarification when the difference could materially affect a claim.

## Claim ledger

For each new or materially strengthened claim, retain:

| Proposed claim | Source | Evidence class | Scope or caveat | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Use status values: ready to use, confirm first, unsupported, or conflict unresolved.

Traceability may appear in analysis notes rather than the resume itself. Do not clutter the final resume with citations unless requested.

## Metrics and estimates

- Never invent metrics or derive them from unsupported assumptions.
- Preserve units, timeframe, baseline, population, and whether the result was individual or team-level.
- Use an estimate only when the user supplies or approves it as an estimate and the wording remains honest.
- Prefer a supported qualitative outcome when no reliable metric exists.

## Final verification

Check each changed claim for employer, title, date, action, ownership, method, scope, outcome, and metric. If any material element lacks adequate support, weaken the wording, label it for confirmation, or omit it.
