# Due-Diligence Questions

Generate questions from material unknowns and risks in the assessment. Prioritize questions whose answers could change whether the user pursues or accepts the role. Do not provide an exhaustive generic list.

## Recruiter or search partner

- What outcomes define success in the first 6–12 months?
- Which listed qualifications are hard screens, and where is there flexibility?
- What are the reporting line, team scope, location, travel, and work-arrangement expectations?
- What compensation range and mix have been approved?
- Why is the role open, and what is the hiring timeline?

## Hiring manager

- What decisions will this person own, influence, or recommend?
- Which problems are most urgent, and what has prevented progress?
- How will priorities be set when strategic and operational demands conflict?
- What resources, budget, team, and executive sponsorship are available?
- How do you provide direction, feedback, and accountability to senior leaders?
- What would cause a strong hire to struggle in this environment?

## Peers and stakeholders

- Where does this role depend on influence rather than formal authority?
- Which cross-functional relationships are healthy, strained, or still being built?
- How are disagreements and tradeoffs resolved?
- What behavior is rewarded in practice?
- What does the job description understate about the day-to-day work?

## Team or direct reports

- What does the team need most from its next leader?
- Where are capability, capacity, morale, or role-clarity gaps?
- How are performance, development, and decision rights handled?
- Which commitments or changes are already in motion?

## Risk and verification

- Ask for examples when answers remain abstract.
- Test claims across interviewers when the issue is material.
- Distinguish evidence from sales language and personal impressions.
- Avoid questions readily answered by supplied materials unless confirmation is itself valuable.
- Do not ask compensation, demographic, medical, family, or protected-status questions on an employer's behalf; frame the user's own constraints only as necessary for their decision.

## Output

Order questions by decision value. For each high-priority question, optionally note:

- the uncertainty it tests,
- the best person to ask,
- what a reassuring or concerning answer would imply.
