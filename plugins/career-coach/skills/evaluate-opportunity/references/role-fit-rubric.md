# Role-Fit Rubric

Assess both directions separately. A qualified candidate can still face a poor opportunity, and an attractive role can still be unattainable.

## Parse the opportunity

Identify:

- Mandate and likely first-year outcomes
- Central responsibilities and decision authority
- Required versus preferred qualifications
- Leadership, functional, technical, and domain expectations
- Reporting relationships, team, budget, and organizational stage when stated
- Repeated themes and likely screening criteria
- Compensation, location, travel, and schedule when stated
- Ambiguities, promotional claims, and missing operating context

Weight explicit requirements, central outcomes, and repeated themes more heavily than generic boilerplate. Do not treat every bullet equally.

## Candidate-to-role fit

For each material requirement, record:

| Requirement | Importance | Candidate evidence | Evidence class | Match | Unknown or gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use these match labels:

- **Demonstrated:** Direct, relevant evidence at adequate scope.
- **Transferable:** Evidence from a different context with a clear mechanism of transfer.
- **Plausible, unconfirmed:** Possible but dependent on missing facts.
- **Gap:** Evidence exists but does not meet the requirement, or the capability is absent.
- **Unknown:** Available sources do not establish a conclusion.

Do not convert transferable or plausible evidence into a direct match. Consider whether a stated requirement is likely a hard screen, learnable after hire, or negotiable, but label this as inference unless established.

## Role-to-candidate fit

Assess the opportunity against known criteria:

- Desired impact, problems, and stakeholders
- Work mix and day-to-day responsibilities
- Scope, authority, and leadership level
- Manager, decision culture, autonomy, and accountability
- Industry, mission, organization stage, and pace
- Compensation, location, travel, schedule, and family constraints
- Risk, stability, reversibility, and ethical concerns
- Learning, reputation, relationships, and future options

Classify each as aligned, acceptable, concern, conflict, or unknown. A conflict with a requirement can outweigh strong candidate qualifications.

## Evidence and confidence

Classify claims as:

- **Explicit fact:** Directly stated in a supplied source or by the user.
- **Supported inference:** Strongly implied by identified facts.
- **Hypothesis:** Plausible but unestablished.
- **Unknown:** Insufficient evidence.

Base confidence on evidence coverage, source quality, unresolved conflicts, and the importance of unknowns. Explain the drivers; do not create false precision.

## Recommendation

Choose one:

- **Pursue:** Evidence supports attainability and alignment; remaining unknowns are manageable.
- **Pursue conditionally:** Attractive enough to investigate, with named conditions or risks.
- **Gather information first:** Material unknowns could reverse the decision.
- **Deprioritize:** Misalignment, hard gaps, or opportunity cost outweigh likely benefit.

State what evidence would most change the recommendation.
