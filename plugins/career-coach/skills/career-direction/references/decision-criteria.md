# Decision Criteria

Use this framework to turn discovery into explicit choices without manufacturing precision.

## Classify criteria

Place each criterion in one category:

- **Requirement:** Failure makes an option infeasible.
- **Strong preference:** Materially affects attractiveness but can be traded.
- **Flexible preference:** Useful as a tiebreaker.
- **Experiment:** A hypothesis that needs evidence before becoming a criterion.
- **Unknown:** Important but not yet understood.

Record whether each item is user-stated, supported by repeated examples, hypothesized, or unresolved.

## Compare alternatives

For each plausible path, assess:

1. Expected impact and work content
2. Scope, authority, and leadership mix
3. Manager and operating environment
4. Industry, mission, and stakeholder fit
5. Compensation, location, travel, and lifestyle
6. Risk and reversibility
7. Learning and career capital
8. Attainability and transition cost

Use qualitative ratings by default: strong fit, acceptable, concern, unknown, or disqualifier. Use numerical weights only when the user requests them and has supplied meaningful priorities. Do not sum unknowns as neutral values.

## Resolve tradeoffs

- Name the competing values explicitly.
- Identify which choice is reversible and which creates path dependence.
- Ask what downside the user would most regret.
- Separate a true constraint from fear, habit, or an untested assumption.
- Propose an experiment, conversation, or current research when information can resolve the tradeoff.

## Career-direction profile

When useful, produce:

- Decision and time horizon
- Desired impact and problems
- Plausible roles and levels
- Preferred work mix, scope, and authority
- Preferred manager and operating environment
- Industries or organizational contexts
- Compensation, location, travel, and risk parameters
- Requirements, strong preferences, and flexible preferences
- Strengths and career capital to leverage
- Development priorities and transition risks
- Working hypotheses and unresolved questions
- Next experiments or decisions

Keep the profile concise enough to reuse in later opportunity evaluations.

## Canonical record structure

Use this structure for `career_direction_record.md` or its selected equivalent. Omit empty sections rather than inventing content.

1. Record identity, last-updated date, source notes, and current decision horizon
2. Desired impact, problems, stakeholders, and work content
3. Plausible roles, levels, paths, and working career hypotheses
4. Preferred scope, authority, leadership mix, manager, and operating environment
5. Industries, missions, organizational contexts, location, travel, compensation, and risk parameters
6. Criteria grouped as requirements, strong preferences, flexible preferences, experiments, and unknowns
7. Strengths and career capital to leverage, development priorities, transition risks, and future options to preserve
8. Supporting examples and evidence labels: user-stated, example-supported pattern, working hypothesis, or unresolved
9. Contradictions, unresolved questions, next experiments, and decisions to revisit

Update the selected record in place. Retain material prior context when a criterion changes, mark the current interpretation and date, and do not create per-skill copies.
