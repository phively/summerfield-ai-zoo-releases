# Discovery Framework

Use this bank selectively. It is not a checklist or interview cadence. Before selecting a question, apply the question gate in `SKILL.md`; ask only when the answer is likely to materially improve the reusable career-direction profile or future guidance. Prefer an uncovered dimension over deeper detail on a settled topic. Stop without a follow-up when no question qualifies.

When a particular opening prompts discovery, translate the issue into a durable criterion that can guide multiple future opportunities. Leave questions about that employer or opening to `evaluate-opportunity`. Revisit an established topic only to resolve a meaningful contradiction, ambiguity, or decision-relevant gap.

## Immediate decision

- What decision are you trying to make, and by when?
- What options are already under consideration?
- What would make this work useful today: clearer criteria, plausible targets, a decision, or next experiments?

## Positive and negative evidence

- Which past responsibilities made you want more of that kind of work? What specifically was energizing?
- Which experiences were successful but personally undesirable?
- Which problems, stakeholders, and outcomes felt most meaningful?
- Which recurring frustrations came from the work itself, the environment, the manager, or a temporary circumstance?
- Which accomplishments are you proud of, and which would you not want to repeat?

## Impact and work content

- What decisions do you want authority to make?
- What scale and type of impact matters: organizational, commercial, public, customer, technical, or team?
- What mix of strategy, execution, people leadership, stakeholder influence, and functional depth is desirable?
- How much ambiguity, turnaround pressure, and operational responsibility is acceptable?
- Which industries, missions, products, customers, or problems are attractive or unacceptable?

## Leadership and operating environment

- Under which managers did you perform best, and what did they consistently do?
- Which management behaviors reduced your effectiveness?
- What balance of autonomy, feedback, direction, and accountability works best?
- What team size, organizational stage, decision pace, and level of structure fit you?
- What cultures or incentive systems have brought out your best and worst work?

## Practical constraints and risk

- What location, remote-work, travel, relocation, schedule, or family constraints are fixed for the next 12–24 months?
- What are the required, target, and aspirational levels for base pay, total cash, equity, benefits, and long-term upside?
- How much income variability, equity risk, organizational instability, or transition time is acceptable?
- Which constraints are non-negotiable, and which could change for an exceptional opportunity?

## Career capital and horizon

- Which capabilities, relationships, reputation, or credentials should the next role build?
- What future options should the next move preserve or create?
- What would make a prestigious opportunity a poor choice?
- Which gaps could reasonably be closed before or during the next move?

## Interpretation rules

- Treat a single example as a clue, not a durable preference.
- Look for patterns across contexts before generalizing.
- Distinguish dislike of a task from dislike of how it was managed.
- Distinguish present constraints from permanent identity claims.
- Reflect tentative interpretations as hypotheses and invite correction.
